This deck is archived to a **public website**
(`https://romeoarch.github.io/my-presentation/`). Publishing is a different legal
act from presenting: showing an image to a room is not publication, putting it on
a public URL is. Everything shipped in `assets/` is therefore either ours, openly
licensed, or used in a way that stands on its own.

## Memes — described, not reproduced

The memes slide carries our own written descriptions rather than the images, plus
a **"See the meme ↗"** link on any card that has a real URL for the original. We
don't hold rights to the images themselves and can't clear them. The commentary
is ours and is fine to publish.

Linking is deliberate and it is the safe half of this: a hyperlink is not a
reproduction, so it needs no licence — the image is served from someone else's
server, to which they control access. Reproducing it here would be a copy on our
server; pointing at it is not. So people still get to see the actual meme, and we
publish nothing we don't own.

Per-meme permalinks go in the `url` field in `content.js`. A card with no `url`
renders **no link at all** — deliberately. A "See the meme" button that lands on
a site's front page promises the specific image and doesn't deliver, which is
worse than saying nothing. Point it at the actual post (the Reddit or X thread it
came from is usually the most stable target, and it credits the poster).

Austria's § 42f UrhG does carry a caricature/parody/pastiche exception, and a
commented meme in a talk has a decent claim to it — but "decent claim" is a thing
you argue *after* a letter arrives, and the realistic cost in Austria isn't a
lawsuit, it's an *Abmahnung*: a cease-and-desist with a lawyer's fee attached.
Not worth it for four tiles.

**When presenting:** tell the joke from the card, and pull the image up live in a
browser if you want the visual. Live display is not publication.

**When archiving:** never add a `src:` to `memes[]` or an `image:` to
`coverMeme` pointing at a picture found online. The cover meme is drawn from
code (`rows` / `skull` / `footer`) for this reason — it carries its own `url`
so it links out to the original post without reproducing it.

**Pick link targets that work logged-out.** Reddit and X posts resolve for
anyone. Facebook group posts and LinkedIn feed permalinks often hit a login
wall, so a visitor to the public archive may not reach the image.

## Indexing

The archived decks carry `noindex, nofollow, noimageindex, noarchive` in the
`<head>` of each deck's `index.html`, injected automatically by
`scripts/publish-deck.ps1`. Third-party image-rights enforcement runs on
reverse-image crawlers, so staying out of the index removes the discovery path
almost entirely. This is a belt-and-braces measure on top of the above, not a
substitute for it.

There is deliberately **no `robots.txt`**: it is only read from the domain root
(`romeoarch.github.io/robots.txt`), and this site is served from the
`/my-presentation/` subpath, so one placed here would be ignored. The meta tag is
the mechanism that actually works — and it is the stronger one anyway, since
`robots.txt` blocks crawling but does not remove a URL from an index.
